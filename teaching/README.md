# Teach or study the switching-tank sequence

Begin with one counterintuitive observation: a rate can jump while the quantity is continuous. Open the [chapter guide](../chapters/README.md) and work in two parts. Sketch the rate and derive slopes first, then compare the graph, exact volume and quadrature. The [Blueprint](https://github.com/nikbearbrown/ai-plus-one-paper/blob/1758bc31d49e499bf50df530aebba3638baab74e/book-queue/editorial-blueprints/22-calculus-ai-one.md) proposes learning outcomes; this is not an institutional syllabus or evidence of classroom use.

Use the [worksheet](../practice/worksheet.md) before revealing the key. Collect the first trace. Then release the [new controller](../practice/worksheet.md#release-after-the-first-attempt) without the key and ask for a short independently defended result. Keep the checked revision with the initial attempt.

| Criterion | What to demonstrate | Points |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Graph and limits | Units, interval, one-sided limits and actual sample are explicit | 2 |
| Derivation | Correct difference quotient and distinction between flow and volume | 2 |
| Accumulation | Continuous volume, correct slopes and exact benchmark | 3 |
| Numerical evidence | Both approximation errors and signed explanation of the jump | 2 |
| Decision and transfer | Candidate table, endpoints and both transfer maximizers | 2 |
| Record | Initial attempt, checked revision and assistance disclosure | 1 |

AI is optional and limited to critiquing an already written step or proposing an extra sample. Do not provide private work or student records to external tools. If used, record the suggestion and identify the tool; if not, mark no AI. Carry out your own differentiating, signed area check or calculation table before accepting anything. A peer with the same question is the equivalent route. The unseen transfer is done unaided.
